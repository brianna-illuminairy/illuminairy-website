The Diagnostic

What the diagnostic shows

Overall performance (June 17, 2026)

Full-length diagnostic, all four modules complete. Total score: 1380 to 1430. Reading and Writing: 670 to 690 (Module 1: 25/27, Module 2: 22/27). Math: 710 to 740 (Module 1: 21/22, Module 2: 17/22).

Math is stronger than Reading and Writing. By difficulty: Reading and Writing 47/54 (88% easy, 87% medium, 87% hard). Math 38/44 (100% easy, 88% medium, 73% hard).

Adaptive routing risk

The SAT is adaptive. Performance on Module 1 decides which Module 2 she gets. She earned the harder Module 2 in both sections, which is good. But she missed 4 easy and medium questions in Reading and Writing. If she performs similarly on test day, she could miss the cutoff for the hard Module 2 in Reading and Writing. That can cap her score for the whole section by up to 130 points.

Protecting Module 1 accuracy on easy and medium Reading and Writing questions is a first priority, not just hard questions at the end.

1. Transitions

Priority 1

Module 1 Q22 (easy): she chose "For example," but the second sentence contradicts the first. Correct: "In contrast." She changed this answer during review from likely-correct to wrong. That is her single most expensive miss in the section.

Module 2 Q23 (medium): she chose "Additionally," but the sentence moves to a final interpretation. Correct: "Ultimately."

How we fix it: label the relationship before reading the choices: contrast, example, result, addition, conclusion, or alternative. Then pick the word that matches.

2. Boundaries

Priority 2

Module 1 Q18 (medium): she chose a semicolon plus participle phrase ("radioactivity; earning"). The correct answer added "and she earned," making a second full clause.

Module 2 Q20 (hard): similar incomplete-clause issue.

How we fix it: at every punctuation choice, check whether each side is a complete sentence on its own.

3. Command of Evidence

Priority 3

Module 2 Q12 and Q14 (both hard): she picked answers that were factually tied to the passage but not matched to the specific claim. On the CO2 table she focused on process emissions when the claim was about total emissions. On the Kurosawa question she picked a true fact about scholars when the claim needed evidence about his own hybrid approach.

How we fix it: restate the exact claim before reading the choices. Reject anything that is true but off-claim.

4. Subject-verb agreement

Priority 4

Module 2 Q22 (medium): she chose "has remained," matching the nearby singular name Henrietta Swan Leavitt. The real subject was the plural "observations."

How we fix it: cross out the long descriptive phrase in the middle and match the verb to the real subject.

Across every Reading and Writing miss, the core issue is the same: she chooses answers that are locally plausible but do not satisfy the full sentence or the full claim.

5. Calculator discipline and factoring

Math priority 1

She is strong with Desmos, but she runs to the calculator on problems that need algebra by hand. Three of six math misses are factoring or factor-theorem questions.

Module 2 Q13: 9x³ - 6x² - 24x, 3x + k is a factor. Factor: 3x(3x² - 2x - 8) = 3x(x - 2)(3x + 4), so k = 4. She graphed and entered 13.15 instead.

Module 2 Q20: factor theorem gives a = 3/2. She answered 5/2. Module 1 Q9: she spent 333 seconds on a nonlinear equation that needed factoring and zero-product form.

How we fix it: when she sees "is a factor of," that is a by-hand trigger. Drill GCF first, factor by grouping, ac-method, factor theorem with substitution, and zero-product property until automatic.

6. Area and volume setup

Math priority 2

Module 2 Q12: cube volume 125,000, find surface area. Side = cube root of 125,000 = 50. Surface area = 6 × 50² = 15,000. She answered 25,000. Cube volume and surface-area relationships are not on the SAT formula sheet.

She got every circle question right. This is specifically an area-and-volume setup gap, not geometry across the board.

How we fix it: focused geometry concept refresh so she has the setup, not only the formula sheet and calculator.

7. Hard algebra precision

Later in plan

Module 2 Q16: three collinear points, set two slopes equal and simplify to ak + bh = hk. She picked a manipulation that did not simplify correctly.

How we fix it: same precision habit as Reading and Writing: set up correctly before manipulating.

8. Multi-step proportional reasoning

Closer to test day

Module 2 Q22: profit and loss question (sell part at a loss, find profit percent needed on the rest to net 20%). Very teachable, but these appear less often on the SAT. Scheduled after factoring is solid.

Test-taking behavior

Keep: fast pace, mark for review, Desmos when the problem suits it, formula sheet, scratch paper when needed.

Adjust: she finished early and reviewed every Reading and Writing question. On the one answer she changed (Module 1 Q22), she went from right to wrong. One careful pass; mark for review only when truly unsure; bank leftover time for Math.

Adjust: default to calculator on algebra problems. Put the calculator down when the question type requires factoring or structure recognition.

The Plan

How we work

Weekly tutoring rhythm (3 sessions)

SessionWhat happens
Session 1Lesson + reteach missed diagnostic questions + hard-only practice set on the week's focus skill
Session 2Same skill family, harder variants + independent practice until 4 correct in a row + maintenance block (~15-20 hard questions) on the other section
Session 3Timed full module (focus section) + review every miss

Weeks alternate between Reading and Writing focus and Math focus, starting with Reading and Writing. On focus weeks: 2 sessions on the focus section (hard questions only) plus 1 maintenance block on the other section. Between sessions: ~110 practice questions per week, ~1,000 total by test day.

Every skill is taught the same way:

1. LearnWe explain the skill and how to spot it.
2. WatchShe watches us solve two examples out loud.
3. Solve togetherShe tries one with us guiding her.
4. Solve with hintsShe leads; we step in only when she is stuck.
5. Solve aloneFour correct in a row to move up a level.
6. Repeat to masteryUntil easy, then medium, then hard is automatic.

We attack skills in priority order. She does not move to the next topic until she hits 95% accuracy on easy, medium, and hard for the current one.
