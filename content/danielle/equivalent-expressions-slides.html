Equivalent expressions:
the patterns to memorize

You know the moves already. This set is about speed: recognizing a factoring pattern on sight so you spend seconds, not minutes. Light on lecture, heavy on examples you work yourself.

Memorize these, and why

The patterns, fastest to spot at the top

  • GCF firstA reflex before anything else. 3x2+12x = 3x(x+4)
  • a2 − b2 = (a−b)(a+b)Difference of squares. Two squares, a minus between them.
  • a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a+b)2Perfect square, positive. Middle term is positive.
  • a2 − 2ab + b2 = (a−b)2Perfect square, negative. Middle term is negative.
  • x2 + bx + c = (x+m)(x+n)Basic trinomial. m+n=b, mn=c.
  • ax2 + bx + cLeading coefficient not 1. Use the AC method.
Why memorize: the SAT hides these inside messier expressions. Recognize the shape instantly and you skip the slow expand-and-regroup work, saving the time you need for the back half of the section.

Reflex · do this before any pattern

Pull out the common factor first

You have this one down already, so just one rep to keep it sharp. Factoring out the shared piece turns ax + b into a(x + ?).

GCF factoring
The expression 12x + 48 is equivalent to a(x + b), where a and b are constants. What is the value of ab?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
1GCF of 12 and 48 is 12: 12x + 48 = 12(x + 4).
2So a = 12, b = 4.
3ab = 12 · 4 = 48. Choice B.

Pattern 1 · difference of squares

Two squares with a minus between them

The identity
a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b)
x2 − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4)  ·  (2x − 3)(2x + 3) = 4x2 − 9
  • Factor: two perfect squares with a minus, like x2−16, become (x−4)(x+4).
  • Expand: a pair like (2x−3)(2x+3) collapses to 4x2−9.
  • Simplify fractions: factor top and bottom, then cancel the shared piece.

Practice · intermediate

Difference of squares
Which of the following is equivalent to 9(x−2)2 − 4(2x+1)2?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Write each term as one square: 9(x−2)2 = (3x−6)2 and 4(2x+1)2 = (4x+2)2.
2Now it is (3x−6)2 − (4x+2)2, with a = 3x−6, b = 4x+2.
3Apply (a+b)(a−b): (3x−6+4x+2)(3x−6−4x−2). The minus hits both 4x and 2.
4Choice A. Choice B forgets the factor of 2 from √4 and uses 2x+1 instead of 4x+2.

1Factor the top: 4x2 − 9 = (2x − 3)(2x + 3).

Careful · is this really a difference of squares?

Looks like squares, but it is a common factor
Which of the following is equivalent to 6(x−5)2 − 3(2x−7)(x−5)?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Both terms share (x−5). Factor it out: (x−5)[6(x−5) − 3(2x−7)].
2Inside: 6(x−5) = 6x−30 and 3(2x−7) = 6x−21.
36x−30 − (6x−21) = 6x−30−6x+21 = −9.
4Result: (x−5)(−9) = −9(x−5). Choice A. Choice B is only the second term by itself, dropping the first.
The lesson: the squared binomial makes this look like a squares problem, but there is no minus between two squares. The shared (x−5) means common factor first.

Pattern 2 · perfect square trinomials

First and last are squares, middle is 2ab

Positive middle
a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a + b)2
x2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)2
Negative middle
a2 − 2ab + b2 = (a − b)2
x2 − 6x + 9 = (x − 3)2
The tell: first and last terms are perfect squares, and the middle equals 2 times their roots. The sign of the middle term is the sign inside the binomial.

Practice · solve for the ratio

The expansion to match against
(px − q)2 = p2x2 − 2pqx + q2
Square-root the leading number for p, read q from the binomial, and the middle is forced.
Perfect square · matching
If 121t2 + mt + 64 = (kt − 8)2, what is the value of mk?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Expand: (kt − 8)2 = k2t2 − 16kt + 64.
2Match leading terms: k2 = 121, so k = 11.
3Match middle terms: m = −16k = −176.
4m/k = −176 / 11 = −16. Choice A. The +64 confirms (−8)2 = 64.

Pattern 3 · basic trinomial

Find two numbers, but read the signs first

The setup
x2 + bx + c = (x + m)(x + n)
where m + n = b and mn = c. Example: x2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).
  • c positive, b positive: both numbers positive.
  • c positive, b negative: both numbers negative.
  • c negative: one positive, one negative. The bigger one carries the sign of b.
Use it to scan: decide the sign pattern first, then read the answer choices. Often only one choice even has the right signs, and you are done before factoring.

Practice · both numbers negative

Basic trinomial

Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution

2Positive product, negative sum, so both numbers are negative. That kills choice B at a glance.

Pattern 4 · leading coefficient not 1

The AC method

For ax2 + bx + c: multiply a · c, find two numbers that multiply to that and add to b, split the middle term, then factor by grouping.

1Factor 6x2 + 31x + 35. Compute ac = 6 · 35 = 210.
2Two numbers multiply to 210, add to 31: that is 10 and 21.
3Split the middle: 6x2 + 10x + 21x + 35.
4Group: 2x(3x + 5) + 7(3x + 5) = (2x + 7)(3x + 5).
On multiple choice, there is a shortcut: pick a small value for x, plug it into the original and each choice, and keep the one that matches. Often faster than factoring.

Speed · when the product is large

Big numbers like 560: narrow before you search

  • Sign first. Product positive with a positive sum means both factors positive; positive product with a negative sum means both negative; negative product means one of each. This cuts the search in half before you start.
  • Start near √ac. The two numbers sit on either side of the square root, so begin there and step outward toward the sum you need.
1Factor 7x2 + 66x + 80. ac = 7 · 80 = 560. Both signs positive, so both numbers positive.
2√560 ≈ 23.7. Scan pairs near there for a sum of 66: 20·28 (48), 16·35 (51), 14·40 (54), 10·56 (66). Stop at 10 and 56.
3Split and group: 7x2 + 10x + 56x + 80 = x(7x + 10) + 8(7x + 10) = (x + 8)(7x + 10).
Stuck on the pair? The two numbers solve t2 − bt + ac = 0. Here t2 − 66t + 560 = 0 gives t = 10 or 56. If that has no whole-number answer, it will not factor cleanly.

The order to run every time

Your fast decision tree

  • 0 · GCFDoes every term share a factor? Pull it out first.
  • 1 · Two squares, minusa2 − b2 → (a−b)(a+b)
  • 2 · Squares + middle 2abPerfect square trinomial. The middle sign is the sign in the binomial.
  • 3 · x2+bx+cFind m, n. Read the signs first to prune the choices.
  • 4 · ax2+bx+cAC method, sign and √ac to move fast. On multiple choice, plug in a number.

How to read signs and move fast

Signs for x2 + bx + c
  • c positive, b positive: both numbers positive.
  • c positive, b negative: both numbers negative.
  • c negative: one positive, one negative. The bigger one carries the sign of b.
Speed moves
  • Plug in a number. On any "which is equivalent" question, pick a small x, evaluate the original and each choice, keep the match.
  • Big ac: decide the signs first, then search for the pair near √ac.
  • Stuck on the pair? Solve t2 − bt + ac = 0. Whole-number roots are the split numbers.
Run them in order: GCF, then difference of squares, then perfect square trinomial, then x2+bx+c, then ax2+bx+c.
