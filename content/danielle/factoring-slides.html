The idea

What factoring is

Factoring means writing something as a product, meaning things multiplied together. It is the reverse of expanding.

Expanding: (x + 2)(x + 3) → x² + 5x + 6
Factoring: x² + 5x + 6 → (x + 2)(x + 3)
Why it works

Why we factor

The zero-product property: if two things multiply to zero, one of them must be zero.

A · B = 0 → A = 0 or B = 0

So if you write a quadratic as (___)(___) = 0, each piece set to zero gives you a solution.

The one ruleMove everything to one side so it equals zero before you factor to solve.
Type 1 of 4

Pull out the common factor

Find something in every term and pull it to the front.

6x² + 9x → 3x(2x + 3)
4x² − 12x → 4x(x − 3)
This was her Question 11Both sides share (y − 42), so pull it out: (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)] = 0, giving y = 42 and y = c + 1.

Always check for a common factor first. It makes everything after it easier.

Type 2 of 4

Simple trinomials

When the number in front of x² is 1, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

x² + 7x + 12 → 3 and 4 → (x + 3)(x + 4)
x² + x − 90 → 10 and −9 → (x + 10)(x − 9)
x² − 4x − 5 → −5 and 1 → (x − 5)(x + 1)

If c is negative, the two numbers have opposite signs.

Type 3 of 4

Harder trinomials: the AC method

When the number in front of x² is not 1. Worked on her 3x² − 19x + 6.

  • ✶Multiply a times c: 3 · 6 = 18.
  • ✶Two numbers that multiply to 18 and add to −19: −18 and −1.
  • ✶Split the middle: 3x² − 18x − 1x + 6.
  • ✶Group and pull out each common factor: 3x(x − 6) − 1(x − 6).
  • ✶(3x − 1)(x − 6)
A common trap

When the x² term is negative

Factor out −1 first, then factor what is left. Her −3x² + 5x + 8:

−3x² + 5x + 8 → −(3x² − 5x − 8)
→ −(3x − 8)(x + 1)

Solutions: x = 8/3 and x = −1.

Type 4 of 4

Two patterns to memorize

Difference of squares: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

x² − 49 = (x − 7)(x + 7)
4x² − 25 = (2x − 5)(2x + 5)

Perfect square: a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)²

x² + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)²
Why it mattersSpotting the perfect square is what makes question #19 fast.
The routine

The recipe, every time

  • ✶Move everything to one side so it equals zero.
  • ✶Pull out a common factor if there is one.
  • ✶Pick the pattern: simple trinomial, AC method, or special.
  • ✶Find the numbers: multiply to ___, add to ___.
  • ✶Write the factors, then check by expanding.
  • ✶Set each factor to zero, if you are solving.
