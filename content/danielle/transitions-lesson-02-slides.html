What the misses have in common
The big finding

Cause & effect is the bottleneck.

It came up in three of your six misses. Twice you didn't spot it when it was the right answer. Once you reached for it when the answer was something else.

When you missed it

Yeats: picked "Likewise" (addition). Answer: "Consequently" (cause & effect).

Stoclet House: picked "Subsequently" (sequence). Answer: "Therefore" (cause & effect).

When you over-applied it

Bauhaus: picked "Therefore" (cause & effect). Answer: "In fact" (reinforcement).

The other three misses all share a smaller pattern: you forced a relationship onto sentences that don't actually have it. We'll fix all of this with one new framework and seven specific walk-throughs.

New framework
Expanding the four categories from lesson 01

What is the second sentence doing?

Last time we covered four categories. To get medium right consistently, you need to split a couple of them. There are seven jobs the second sentence can do. Each job has its own family of words.

Job 01
Push back
however, nonetheless, granted, that said, yet, still, on the other hand
Job 02
Pile on
moreover, furthermore, also, likewise, similarly, in addition
Job 03 · NEW
Intensify
in fact, indeed
Job 04
Result from
therefore, consequently, thus, accordingly, hence, as a result
Job 05
Exemplify
for example, for instance, specifically, in particular
Job 06 · NEW
Restate
in other words, that is
Job 07
Sequence
then, next, subsequently, eventually, finally, ultimately, meanwhile
The two new ones

Intensify and Restate get their own slots now.

"In fact" and "indeed" don't just add information, they double down on the previous claim. "In other words" and "that is" don't add either, they say the same thing in plainer words. Mixing these up with Pile On or Result From is exactly what happened on Bauhaus.

✦Illuminairy
Sharpening the new jobs
Pile on vs Intensify

Two ways to "keep going in the same direction."

Pile on (Addition)

The library doubled its book holdings. Moreover, it tripled its digital media.

Two separate facts about the library. Sentence two adds something new on top of sentence one. Both true, but they're different facts.

Intensify (Reinforcement)

The library has grown significantly. In fact, it has doubled in size over the past decade.

Sentence two is the same idea as sentence one, just with stronger evidence. It doesn't add a new fact, it backs up the first claim with proof.

The test that separates them

Does sentence 2 prove sentence 1, or add a second point next to it?

Proof → Intensify (in fact, indeed). Second point → Pile on (moreover, also, in addition). The Bauhaus question hinged exactly on this distinction.

✦Illuminairy
Sharpening Result From
Your weakest spot

Cause & effect needs a real cause.

"Therefore," "consequently," and "thus" only work if sentence one literally causes sentence two. Not "leads to," not "is related to." Causes.

The "so" test

Replace the blank with the word "so." If the sentence reads naturally, cause & effect is the relationship. If "so" feels forced, it's a different job.

Test 1 — Yeats

Yeats was fascinated by mysticism, so his poems are deeply symbolic.

Reads naturally. Cause & effect.

Test 2 — Stoclet House

The Stoclet House is preserved down to its original furniture, so it qualifies for UNESCO status.

Reads naturally. Cause & effect.

Test 3 — Bauhaus (the trap)

The Bauhaus reshaped architecture, so its principles still influence today.

Reads okay, but loosely. The second sentence isn't really a result, it's another way of saying the first sentence is still true. That's Intensify (in fact), not Result From.

Walk-through 01 · Set 1, Q19
The only easy one you missed

Billy Joel.

Easy Transitions 155 sec on this · target ~25 sec

Billy Joel is a singer and songwriter who is best known for the song "Piano Man." He started his music career in earnest when he found he did not have enough credits to graduate high school and claimed he did not care about finishing his education. twenty-five years later he contacted his former school and wrote essays that allowed him to belatedly receive a degree.

A
Inevitably,
B
Simultaneously,
C
Consequently,
D
Nonetheless,
Plain words

Sentence A: He dropped out and said he didn't care about a diploma.
Sentence B: 25 years later he went back and did the work to get it.

What's the second sentence doing?

He said he didn't care. He then did the opposite of what that suggests. Sentence two pushes back on sentence one. Push back = contrast/concession. The answer is in the "Nonetheless" family.

Why "Inevitably" tripped you

"Inevitably" means "it was bound to happen." But nothing in sentence one suggests he was bound to come back for the degree, the opposite. He said he didn't care. Him doing it 25 years later is a reversal, not an inevitability. Memorize this: inevitably = it was always going to happen. If the second sentence is a surprise, "inevitably" is wrong.

Quick eliminations on the rest

B "Simultaneously" means at the same time. The events are 25 years apart, so this is impossible. C "Consequently" = caused by. His dropping out didn't cause him to come back. It was the opposite, he came back despite his earlier attitude. Both gone.

Walk-through 02 · Set 2, Q8
Missed cause & effect #1

Yeats.

Medium Transitions 25 sec on this

William Butler Yeats is considered one of the foremost poets of the 20th century. He was influential in the Irish Literary Revival, and he was also deeply fascinated by mysticism and astrology. many of his poems are deeply symbolic and have allusive imagery.

A
However,
B
Likewise,
C
Notwithstanding,
D
Consequently,
Plain words

Sentence A: Yeats was fascinated by mysticism and astrology.
Sentence B: His poems are full of symbols and allusions.

Apply the "so" test

"Yeats was fascinated by mysticism, so his poems are symbolic." Reads naturally. That's cause & effect. His interest in mysticism is the cause; the symbolic style of his poems is the effect.

Why "Likewise" tripped you

"Likewise" means "in the same way." It's a Pile On word. It would work if sentence two were a second separate fact about Yeats ("Likewise, he was an active politician"). But sentence two isn't a separate fact, it's the consequence of the fascination from sentence one. His symbolic style isn't similar to his mysticism, it's caused by it.

Spot the trap going forward

When two sentences are clearly related and you start to reach for an addition word, run the "so" test first. If "so" fits, the answer is in the Result From family, not Pile On.

Walk-through 03 · Set 2, Q3
Missed cause & effect #2

Stoclet House.

Medium Transitions 194 sec on this · target ~45 sec

Since it was completed in 1911, the Stoclet House in Belgium has been among the finest examples of the Vienna Secession of the Art Nouveau style. The integrity of the exterior architecture and garden remains almost untouched, and the interior has undergone few changes. Even much of the furniture is original. the house fulfills the architectural criteria necessary for a UNESCO World Heritage site, which it became in 2009.

A
Subsequently,
B
Otherwise,
C
Therefore,
D
However,
Plain words

Sentence A: The house is preserved, even the furniture is original.
Sentence B: The house qualifies for UNESCO status.

Apply the "so" test

"The house is fully preserved, so it qualifies for UNESCO." Reads naturally. The preservation is the cause; the UNESCO qualification is the effect.

Why "Subsequently" tripped you (and ate 194 seconds)

The passage mentions two dates (1911 and 2009), so your brain locked onto time order. "Subsequently" means "next in time" — and yes, 2009 is after 1911. But the relationship between the two sentences isn't time, it's logic. The qualification didn't just happen later, it happened because the house met the criteria. Time order is incidental. The logic is causal.

The check that would have saved you

When you find yourself between a Sequence word and a Result From word, ask: "Did the second event happen just because time passed, or because the first event caused it?" If A caused B, use Result From. Time alone is rarely the relationship a transitions question is testing.

Walk-through 04 · Set 2, Q25
Over-applied cause & effect

Bauhaus.

Medium Transitions 233 sec on this · the slowest of any miss

The Bauhaus movement, a revolutionary art and design school founded in Germany, made significant strides in architecture by embracing functionalism and industrial materials. This approach, which emphasized simplicity, efficiency, and the integration of art with technology, reshaped the field of architecture. the Bauhaus's groundbreaking principles continue to influence modern architectural practices even today.

A
Therefore,
B
In contrast,
C
In fact,
D
Nevertheless,
Plain words

Sentence A: The Bauhaus changed architecture.
Sentence B: Its principles still influence architecture today.

What's sentence 2 doing?

Sentence 2 is the same claim as sentence 1, with stronger evidence. Sentence 1 says the Bauhaus changed architecture. Sentence 2 backs that up: yes, in fact, it still does. Job = Intensify.

Why "Therefore" tripped you (and ate 233 seconds)

You were thinking "the Bauhaus changed architecture, therefore its influence continues." That sounds reasonable. But the continued influence isn't really a logical consequence of past influence, it's more evidence for the original claim. The two sentences are making the same point, just hitting it twice. That's not Result From, that's Intensify.

The cleaner test

Ask: "Is sentence 2 a new fact that follows from sentence 1, or is it the same fact restated with more punch?" If new fact → Result From. If same fact with proof → Intensify. The 233 seconds means you were stuck between two interpretations. Run this test and you can settle it in five seconds.

Walk-through 05 · Set 2, Q2
Forced contrast onto a sequence

Baklava.

Medium Transitions · Sequence 37 sec on this · fine timing

Baklava, a sweet and sticky dessert, originates from an eighth century B.C.E delicacy consisting of layered flatbread sprinkled with nuts. This dessert enjoyed by the Assyrian people was later adapted by the Ancient Greek and Roman people by adding honey, cheese, and bay leaves between the layers of bread. the Ottoman Empire brought about the way of making baklava that is closest to today's version around 500 years ago.

A
Consequently,
B
Finally,
C
Regardless,
D
For instance,
Plain words

Sentence A: Assyrians had basic baklava. Greeks and Romans added honey, cheese, bay leaves.
Sentence B: The Ottomans made the version closest to today's around 500 years ago.

What's the structure?

This whole passage is a timeline: Assyrian (8th c. BCE) → Greek/Roman → Ottoman (500 years ago). Sentence B is the last step in that timeline. Job = Sequence. "Finally" is the sequence word that closes a list of steps.

Why "Regardless" tripped you

"Regardless" is a contrast word, it means "in spite of what was just said." But nothing in sentence A is being contradicted or qualified by sentence B. The Ottomans aren't fighting the Greek/Roman version, they're the next step after it. There's no contrast anywhere in this passage, so a contrast word can't be the answer.

The rule this teaches

If you can't point to where the contrast is in the passage, don't pick a contrast word. Same goes for the other categories. The transition has to match a relationship that's actually on the page, not one you imagined.

Walk-through 06 · Set 2, Q11
Conditional logic

Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Medium Transitions 69 sec on this

In the early months of 1942, the Bureau of Labor Statistics issued a warning about a looming challenge that the United States would soon face. prompt action was carried out, the country would confront a severe shortage of six million workers, jeopardizing its productivity by the end of 1943.

A
If
B
While
C
Unless
D
Therefore,
Plain words

Sentence A: BLS warned about a coming problem.
Sentence B: [blank] action is taken, the country will have a six-million worker shortage.

What does the second sentence need?

The shortage happens if action is not taken. So we need a conditional that means "without" or "if not." That's "unless." "Unless prompt action was carried out" = "if prompt action was not carried out."

Why "If" tripped you

"If" is a positive conditional. "If prompt action was carried out, the country would face a shortage" would mean: taking action causes the shortage. That's backwards. Taking action is what prevents the shortage. So "if" fails the logic test.

The if/unless rule

If X happens, Y happens. X causes Y. Positive condition.
Unless X happens, Y happens. Without X, Y happens. X is what would prevent Y.
When the action would stop the bad outcome, use "unless." When the action would cause the outcome, use "if."

Walk-through 07 · Set 2, Q19
Reading the reaction, not the event

Gamma-ray bursts.

Medium Transitions 41 sec on this

For many years, astronomers believed that gamma-ray bursts (GRB) either lasted no longer than 2 seconds or for even less time. they were shocked when two NASA telescopes observed a GRB that lasted for 50 seconds.

A
Understandably,
B
Surprisingly,
C
Interestingly,
D
Conclusively,
Read carefully: what is the blank describing?

The blank introduces sentence B. Sentence B says: "they were shocked when telescopes observed a 50-second GRB." The blank is describing the astronomers' reaction (being shocked), not the discovery itself.

Is their reaction expected or unexpected?

They believed GRBs were at most 2 seconds. They saw one that lasted 50 seconds. Of course they were shocked. Their shock is completely understandable given what they used to believe. So we want a word that says "their reaction makes sense." That's "Understandably."

Why "Surprisingly" tripped you

The discovery was surprising, yes. But the blank isn't describing the discovery, it's describing the astronomers' reaction to it. "Surprisingly, they were shocked" would mean their shock itself was surprising, like, "weirdly, they reacted by being shocked." That's not the meaning the passage wants. The shock is the expected reaction to a surprising finding.

The mini-rule

When a transition word describes how someone reacted, ask whether the reaction is the obvious response given the setup. If yes, use "Understandably" or "Naturally." If the reaction is itself unexpected, then use "Surprisingly" or "Strangely."

Vocab clinic
Tricky transitions you've now seen

Ten transition words to know cold.

These showed up in your misses or in their distractors. Memorize what each one signals.

Nonetheless Push back. Sentence 2 happens in spite of sentence 1. Same family as "nevertheless" and "even so."
In fact Intensify. Sentence 2 backs up sentence 1 with stronger evidence. Same family as "indeed."
Subsequently Sequence. Means "after that, in time." Pure time order. Does not mean "as a result."
Inevitably Sequence + fate. Means "it was bound to happen." Only fits if the passage shows that the outcome was unavoidable.
Understandably Reaction signal. The reaction described in sentence 2 makes sense given sentence 1. "Naturally" is the same.
Granted Push back (soft). Means "I admit this next point, which weakens what I just said." Concession word.
Unless Negative conditional. Means "if not." Use when the action would prevent the outcome.
Regardless Push back. Means "no matter what was just said." Only fits if there's actually something to push back on.
Notwithstanding Push back. Same as "despite this." Sentence 2 happens even though sentence 1 might suggest otherwise.
Otherwise Conditional. Means "if not, then this would happen." Used to introduce what would happen in the opposite scenario.

Lookalike battles
Words that fool you

Three matchups you've now lost.

Subsequently vs Therefore

Both can follow a list of facts about something good or impressive. Subsequently just means "after that in time." Therefore means "as a logical result." Test: did the second thing happen because of the first thing, or just later than it? Stoclet House was preserved therefore it qualified for UNESCO, not subsequently it qualified for UNESCO.

Therefore vs In fact

Both sound like they "continue the same direction." Therefore introduces a new fact that follows logically from the first one. In fact doubles down on the original claim with more evidence. Test: is sentence 2 a new fact, or the same fact backed up? Bauhaus's modern influence isn't a new fact that follows from its past influence, it's the same fact proven again. That's "In fact," not "Therefore."

Likewise vs Consequently

Likewise means "in the same way" — two separate facts that happen to be parallel. Consequently means "as a result" — one thing causes the other. Test: are the two sentences separate parallel facts, or is one the cause of the other? Yeats's fascination with mysticism didn't parallel his symbolic poems, it caused them.

Activity 02 · trap detector
Spot the trap before picking the word

Read each passage. Name the trap.

Passage A

Researchers initially hypothesized that the new species of beetle would be found only in subtropical forests. further fieldwork revealed populations thriving in alpine meadows above 9,000 feet.

WHAT TRAP IS THIS QUESTION TESTING?

Sequence trapPicking "subsequently" when the answer wants a logical relationship
Contrast trapSetup-then-flip pattern, needs "however" or "nonetheless"
Reinforcement trapTwo sentences making the same point, needs "in fact"
Cause trapSentence 2 is the result of sentence 1, needs "therefore"
Contrast trap

"Initially hypothesized" sets up an expectation. "Further fieldwork revealed" knocks it down. Classic setup-then-flip. The word you want is in the "however / nonetheless" family. If you see "initially," "at first," "originally," or "may seem" in sentence 1, expect contrast in sentence 2.

Name the trap.

Passage B

Marie Tharp's seafloor maps were dismissed as "girl talk" by the male geologists of her era, who refused to allow her on research vessels. her work eventually proved the existence of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and reshaped the field of plate tectonics.

WHAT TRAP IS THIS QUESTION TESTING?

Cause trapThe dismissal caused her work to succeed, needs "consequently"
Reinforcement trapSentence 2 backs up sentence 1, needs "in fact"
Contrast / concession trapSetup-then-flip across time, needs "nonetheless" or "still"
Example trapSentence 2 gives an example of sentence 1, needs "for instance"
Contrast / concession trap

The first sentence says she was dismissed and excluded. The second sentence says she succeeded anyway. That's "Nonetheless." The trap here is the cause trap — you might be tempted to read "the dismissal caused her work to succeed" but it didn't. She succeeded despite the dismissal, not because of it. When the second sentence flips the first across time, that's still contrast, not sequence and not cause.

Final round

Name the trap.

Passage C

Bioluminescent algae produce light through a chemical reaction involving the protein luciferin and the enzyme luciferase. this same reaction has been adapted by researchers for use in medical imaging, allowing the tracking of cancer cells inside living tissue.

WHAT TRAP IS THIS QUESTION TESTING?

Cause trapSentence 2 is a logical result of sentence 1, needs "therefore"

Addition trap

Sentence 1 explains the natural reaction. Sentence 2 says researchers also use the reaction. Cause & effect or reinforcement?

Run the "so" test before picking.

Medium Transitions

Octopuses have been observed using coconut shells as portable shelters, carrying them across the seafloor and reassembling them around their bodies when threatened. these animals display one of the most sophisticated tool-use behaviors of any invertebrate, rivaling that of some primates.

A
However,
B
In fact,
C
Therefore,
D
Subsequently,
Walk-through

Sentence A: octopuses carry coconut shells around as shelters. Sentence B: they display sophisticated tool use, rivaling primates. Is sentence B a result of sentence A? No, sentence B is a broader claim that sentence A is evidence for. Sentence B intensifies sentence A by stating its larger significance. That's "In fact."

"So" test: "Octopuses carry coconut shells, so they display sophisticated tool use." Reads loose. The shell-carrying is the tool use, it doesn't cause the tool use. Fail. Not Therefore. Answer is B.

Concession word in disguise

Hard Transitions

Recent studies have demonstrated that bilingual children develop stronger executive function and metalinguistic awareness than their monolingual peers. these advantages have been observed primarily in children raised in environments where both languages are used regularly at home, suggesting that simple exposure to a second language at school may not be sufficient.

A
For instance,
B
Consequently,
C
That said,
D
In fact,
Walk-through

Sentence A: bilingual kids have advantages. Sentence B: but only when both languages are used at home. Sentence B walks back sentence A by adding a limit on when the advantage actually shows up. That's a concession, the soft-contrast family. "That said" is the concession word here.

Eliminations: "For instance" would mean sentence B is an example of the advantage, but it's actually a limit on the advantage. "Consequently" would mean the limit is caused by the advantage, which makes no sense. "In fact" would strengthen sentence A, but sentence B weakens it. Answer is C.

Timing
The other reason medium is hard

If you spend 233 seconds, you're guessing.

Long times on a single transitions question mean you've stopped using the method and started reading each choice over and over to see which "feels right." That's vibe-checking. Vibe-checking is what gets these wrong.

Easy Target time per question. If you're past this, move on with your best read and flag it. ~25s
Medium Same rule. Medium passages are slightly longer so you get a little more. ~45s
Hard Hard questions deserve more time, but not three minutes. ~75s
The hard cap

Two-minute rule: if you've been on a transitions question for two minutes, commit to a choice and move on.

Two minutes is already double the time budget for hard. After that, more thinking does not produce a better answer, it produces a worse one. The method gives you the answer in 30 to 60 seconds or it doesn't. Pick the choice that survives elimination and go.
