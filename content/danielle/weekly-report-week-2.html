Weekly Summary

Session 1 · Tuesday, June 16

This week we focused on transitions. Transitions are a type of Reading and Writing question which provides you with two clauses with a blank between them, and it asks you to pick the transition term or phrase (“Therefore,” “Furthermore,” “In contrast,” and similar) that connects the clauses.

We taught her how to solve these types of questions. First, you read both clauses, then you determine the relationship between them. For instance, does sentence two add on to sentence one or is it in contrast or disagreement with sentence one? We taught her the different categories of relationships that appear on the SAT. Then we taught her the terms that appear on the SAT for each category. From there, she was given a flashcard deck to memorize the terms for each relationship type and assigned practice problems to reinforce what she learned.

Session 2 · Thursday, June 18

Equivalent expressions: 19 of 20 on easy (95%), 18 of 25 on medium (72%), both assignments complete. She kept her week 1 nonlinear equation practice going as well.

  • Cause vs. similar facts. When sentence 2 happens because of sentence 1, Therefore or Consequently fit — not Likewise.
  • Turnaround. When sentence 2 reverses an earlier attitude, Nonetheless fit better than a word that made the outcome sound inevitable.
  • Strengthening. When sentence 2 backs up sentence 1 more strongly, In fact fit — not Therefore or another cause-and-effect word.
  • The “so” test. Before picking a cause-and-effect word, she checks whether “sentence 1, so sentence 2” reads naturally.
