What we covered

Two Reading and Writing sessions: Transitions

Both sessions were hands-on. We used sorting activities, matching drills, and live walk-throughs on the questions she missed on the diagnostic and on her homework.

  • Session 1 (June 16): We taught her to cover the blank, explain in plain words how the two sentences connect (contrast, cause, addition, or similarity), then pick the linking word that fits. We worked through all three transition questions she missed on the diagnostic.
  • Session 2 (June 18): We reviewed her scores on Transitions homework set 1, then worked through medium-level problems together, including several she missed on homework set 2. We also practiced a simple check for cause-and-effect words: if you cannot say "so" between the sentences, the word is not showing cause and effect.
  • Homework path: Slide deck review, flashcards to 95 percent, then homework sets in order (Transitions 1, then 2, then 3).
Aha moments

Where it clicked

  • She explains how the sentences connect before she reads the answer choices. By session two she was covering the blank and saying whether the second sentence contrasts, adds, shows cause, or makes a concession. Then she picks the word that matches.
  • When two answer choices mean the same thing, she crosses both out first. That kept her from picking words that sound right but do not fit the passage.
  • The three transition questions she missed on the diagnostic all made sense on review. Once she walked through how the sentences relate, she could see why each correct answer fit.
  • After we reviewed her homework misses, she scored 96 percent on flashcards. That shows the approach stuck once she saw where she went wrong on medium-level problems.
  • The "so" check fixed several cause-and-effect misses. If she cannot insert "so" between the two sentences, the linking word is not showing cause and effect.

Math homework: equivalent expressions

Where she still loses points on medium problems

Danielle understands the concepts. She scored 95 percent on easy problems and 72 percent on medium. The misses are not random. They fall into four patterns:

  • Getting signs wrong when distributing through parentheses. On one problem she flipped a sign inside a trinomial and got the wrong final answer for a + b.
  • Forgetting to combine x² terms after expanding squared expressions. She got the other parts of the problem right but left 3x² instead of x² because she did not subtract the second squared term.
  • Long multi-step problems where she ran out of steam. She skipped two questions after trying for more than five minutes each. These required expanding, combining, and then factoring.
  • Reading the question too fast and answering for the wrong variable. She often finds the right value but submits the wrong letter, or gives a reciprocal when the question asks for a ratio.

What we will do: Keep assigning medium sets until she hits 95 percent on her own. The gaps are execution (signs, combining like terms, reading what the question asks for), not concept. This week's Math sessions on perfect squares and the quadratic formula build on the same algebra skills.
