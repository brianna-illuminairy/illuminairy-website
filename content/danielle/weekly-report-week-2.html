Aha moments

  • She named the relationship before looking at choices. By session two she was covering the blank and stating what sentence two does (contrast, cause, sequence, concession) before reading any answer options.
  • Copycat elimination landed. When two transition words do the same job, she crosses both out first, then reads the passage. That cut down on trap answers on medium questions.
  • Diagnostic misses came back. Wallace Stevens (Nonetheless / contrast), Cosmic Canvas (sequence from first impression to final understanding), and the rhythmicity study (Granted / concession) all clicked with the method, not memorization.
  • Flashcards after review: 96 percent. After we walked her Transitions 2 misses live, she ran the portal flashcard deck and scored 96 percent. That confirms the method stuck once she saw where the medium traps were.
  • Cause-and-effect trap: The "so" test (if you cannot say "so ___" between the sentences, it is not cause-and-effect) fixed several medium misses from Transitions 2.

Equivalent Expressions: error patterns

Where medium misses cluster (and what to do)

Danielle largely understands factoring and equivalent expressions. Easy is at 95 percent. Medium is at 72 percent. The misses are not random. They fall into four buckets:

  • Sign distribution through parentheses (Q3): Subtracting a trinomial and flipping every sign inside. She answered 3 instead of 1 on a + b because the middle term came out wrong.
  • Squared-binomial expansion, then combine x² terms (Q10): She got the linear term (52x) and constant (25) right but left 3x² instead of x². She expanded the first part but did not subtract the 2x² from the second squared term.
  • Multi-step expand-then-factor (Q12, skipped): Expand 12(x − 3)², distribute, combine to a quadratic, then factor. She stalled here for 334 seconds. Same family as Q16 (skipped): matching coefficients when one side has x/a.
  • Identity / coefficient matching, wrong final answer (Q17, Q18, Q20): She often finds a or b or d correctly but submits the wrong variable (Q18: answered d = 1/2 instead of c = 3/2) or the reciprocal ratio (Q20: answered −16 instead of −1/16 for k/m).

Recommendation: Keep assigning factoring and simplifying quadratic and polynomial expressions until she hits 95 percent at medium independently. The gaps are execution (signs, combining like terms, reading what the question asks for), not concept. Perfect-square and discriminant work this week should reinforce the same expansion and coefficient-matching muscles.
