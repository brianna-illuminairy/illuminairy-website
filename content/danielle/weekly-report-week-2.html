How the SAT scored her diagnostic

Not a classroom percent-correct test

The SAT is adaptive. Each section (Reading and Writing, Math) has two modules. Everyone gets the same Module 1. How she does on Module 1 decides whether Module 2 is the harder version (needed for a top score) or an easier version that caps how high she can score in that section.

The score is not a simple count of right answers divided by 98. The test uses a statistical model (similar to what psychometricians call IRT) that weighs which questions she got right: easy, medium, or hard. Getting hard Module 2 questions right moves the score more than getting easy ones right. That is why we track each skill at easy, medium, and hard in homework, not just overall percent correct.

On June 6 Danielle reached the harder Module 2 in both sections, but it was close. She needs a buffer above the cutoff so one bad day does not drop her to the easier module.
