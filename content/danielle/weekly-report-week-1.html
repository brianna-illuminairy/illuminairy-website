Where she started

Diagnostic baseline, taken June 6

Danielle started at a composite of 1100 to 1150, with Reading and Writing at 580 to 600 and Math at 520 to 550. Math was the weaker section, so that is where we started.

The breakdown pointed straight at factoring and quadratics. She lost the most ground on quadratic problems. On several of them she tried to graph problems that cannot be solved by graphing, and factoring slips showed up across sections. Her hard-question accuracy in Math was 47 percent.

These two topics are worth a lot of points together. Factoring is the foundation under Equivalent Expressions, and quadratics run throughout the Math section, so teaching factoring and the basics of quadratics in the same week covers a large share of the questions she was missing.

What we covered

Two math sessions: factoring and nonlinear equations

We doubled up on math in week one so we could go one level deeper than a single session allows. That paid off with a clear breakthrough on the exact problem types she missed on the diagnostic.

  • When to graph and when to solve by hand. This was the root cause of two of her diagnostic misses. She now recognizes that problems with a second constant or an exact-form answer with a radical should be solved by hand, not graphed.
  • The five nonlinear problem types, with the right approach for each: absolute value, quadratic, rational, square root, and a line crossing a curve.
  • Factoring from the ground up. Factoring as the reverse of multiplying, the zero product property, moving every term to one side, the method for trinomials with a leading coefficient, factoring out a negative one, and the difference-of-squares and perfect-square patterns.
  • Quadratic expression versus equation, and how to spot a quadratic even when the problem is written in non-standard form. This feeds directly into the "equivalent expressions" questions she is now practicing.
  • Live problem work, including factoring 5x³ + 15x² + 10x, using FOIL, factoring a shared expression, and finally solving the diagnostic quadratic that stumped her on test day.
Aha moments

Where it clicked

  • She solved the question that beat her on the diagnostic. The quadratic with a second constant only half made sense to her at the end of session one. By session two she factored out the shared term on her own and worked it all the way to the answer.
  • She taught the method back. Midway through session one she identified an absolute-value problem on sight and stated the full by-hand approach without prompting. That is the same decision that cost her points before.
  • She caught her own mistake. Reviewing a missed problem, she realized she had multiplied where she should have added when combining like terms. Spotting her own error is a sign the process is becoming automatic.
  • A new way to see shared expressions. Rewriting one problem as a fraction so the common term cancels to one was the moment that idea fully landed for her.

  • She learns quickly. She refreshed factoring skills she had not used in years within a single session, and was applying them to real test-format problems by the next one.

The plan from here

What is next

This week, June 15 to 21
Transitions, across two Reading and Writing sessions
This is the single highest-return area in her Reading and Writing. It shows up often, she missed it at every difficulty level, and it is very teachable. Transitions is a closed set of relationships: contrast, continuation, cause and effect, example, and sequence. With a simple method, cover the blank, name the relationship, then match, her easy and medium misses should come back quickly. We expect her to have it down by the end of the week.
Following week, June 22 to 28
Quadratic formula, determinants, and exponents, radicals, and rational exponents
These open the door to intermediate and hard Equivalent Expressions and quadratic questions, which is where her next round of point gains in math will come from.

Doubling up on one area in week one produced a breakthrough, so we plan to keep that cadence: two sessions on the same skill in a week, then move on once it is solid. Danielle also responded better to the more interactive second session than to the lecture-style first one, so we will lean further into hands-on exercises during sessions and adjust only where needed.
