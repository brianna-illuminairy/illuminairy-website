The Diagnostic

What's costing her the most points

First, how the SAT decides her score

The SAT is adaptive. Each section, Reading and Writing and Math, has two parts called modules. Everyone gets the same Module 1. How many questions she gets right in Module 1 decides which Module 2 the test gives her next.

Do well on Module 1 and she gets the harder Module 2, which is the only version that can reach a top score. Do poorly on Module 1 and the test switches her to an easier Module 2, and her score for that whole section gets capped. Once that switch happens, nothing she does in Module 2 can undo it. Getting the easier Module 2 in a section can cost around 130 points.

On her diagnostic, Danielle earned the harder Module 2 in both sections, which is good. But it was close. In Reading and Writing she got 19 of 27 right in Module 1, and the cutoff to earn the harder module is about 18. In Math she got 16 of 22, and the cutoff is about 15. So in each section she was one question away from being dropped to the easier module. The first priority is strengthening her skills so she is consistently several questions above the cutoff.

1. She runs out of time on Math, then rushes the end

+35Very fixable

Math is where her clock breaks. She spent close to four minutes each on three hard questions she actually got right (Module 1 questions 16 and 20, and Module 2 question 5, all over 210 seconds). That time has to come from somewhere. By the end of the Math second module she had run out of it, and she rushed the last two questions in under a minute each and missed both, even though they were a percentage and a ratio question, the kind she got right earlier when they were easier.

Her Reading and Writing pacing is the opposite. The timing chart shows her steady and close to the class average there, so Reading is not a timing problem and we mostly leave her pace alone.

How we fix it: we cap hard Math questions at two minutes, so if she is not close she flags it and moves on instead of sinking four minutes into one. We train instant question recognition (in the plan below) so routine questions take less time and leave room for the hard ones. We drill Math pacing in the final weeks.

2. Area and volume

+30Very fixable

She missed all 3 area and volume questions, including an easy one. This is a formula problem, not a thinking problem.

How we fix it: we make sure she knows each formula cold and has a set method for setting these up. Because the only gap is the formula, these turn into reliable points quickly.

3. Command of evidence

+26Very fixable

She missed 3 of these. One was an easy question she should have gotten; two were hard. These ask her to pick the fact, quote, or data point that best backs up a claim.

How we fix it: we teach one repeatable method. Read the claim, say in her own words what would prove it, then find the answer that matches. A method she runs every time improves fast.

4. Nonlinear functions

+24Quick once she graphs

She missed 2 of these (a medium and a hard). These are parabolas and other curves, where the built in calculator can show the answer.

How we fix it: we teach her to graph these in the calculator and read the answer straight off the graph, like the lowest point or where it crosses zero, instead of solving by hand.

5. Nonlinear equations and systems

+24Steady practice

She missed 2 of these. This is also her most common question type on the whole test; it showed up about 13 times. Because it appears so often, even a small accuracy gain here adds the most points of any single Math skill.

How we fix it: we drill these from medium to hard until she is reliable, and give them extra time in practice since they come up the most.

6. Text structure and purpose

+24Fixable

She missed 3 (a medium and two hard). These ask what job a sentence or paragraph is doing, or how the passage is built.

How we fix it: we teach her to name the job of the sentence in plain words first (it introduces, it gives an example, it disagrees, it concludes), then look at the choices.

7. Transitions

+22Very fixable

She missed 3 transition questions, one easy, one medium, one hard. These ask for the right linking word, like "however" or "therefore."

How we fix it: transitions only come in a few flavors (continue the idea, contrast it, show cause, give an example). We teach her to decide the relationship first, then pick the word. This is one of the fastest skills on the test to fix.

8. Circles

+20Very fixable

She missed 2 of 3 circle questions (a medium and a hard). Like area and volume, the gap is mostly the formulas (area, circumference, and the equation of a circle) plus a method.

How we fix it: we lock in the circle formulas and practice the standard setups until they are routine.

9. Words in context

+18Fixable

She missed 2, both on the harder module. These give a sentence with a blank and ask for the best word.

How we fix it: we teach her to cover the answer choices, come up with her own word from the sentence, then match it.

10. Algebra: the hardest equations and inequalities

+18Fixable

She gets every easy and medium algebra question right. She only missed 2 hard ones (a two variable equation and an inequality). Her foundation is solid; just the hardest versions trip her.

How we fix it: we start her straight at the hard level here, since she does not need the easy practice.

11. Sentence grammar

+16Very fixable

She missed 2 grammar questions, one easy and one medium. The diagnostic calls these "form, structure, and sense," which means subject and verb agreement, verb forms, and how the parts of a sentence fit together. These follow clear rules.

How we fix it: we teach the handful of rules these questions test and drill them until they are automatic. Rule based skills like this move quickly.

Smaller single misses

+18Daily practice

A handful of one off misses add up: one punctuation question (joining or separating two sentences), one factoring question, one comparing two passages, one main idea, and one chart reading. We pick these up in the daily mixed practice instead of spending whole lessons on them.

For the punctuation miss, we pin down the exact rule (comma, semicolon, or colon) from the question she got wrong, then drill that one.

The Plan

How we fix it

We go after the biggest point getters first. For Danielle those are also her quickest wins, so she sees a real jump early and trusts that 1400 is back in reach.

Every skill is taught the same way:

1. LearnWe explain the skill and how to spot it.
2. WatchShe watches us solve two examples out loud.
3. Solve togetherShe tries one with us guiding her.
4. Solve with hintsShe leads; we step in only when she is stuck.
5. Solve aloneFour correct in a row to move up a level.
6. Repeat to masteryUntil easy, then medium, then hard is automatic.

We start each skill at the level where she is actually stuck, not from scratch. On algebra, for example, she already gets the easy and medium questions, so we jump straight to the hard ones and save the time.

Cutting the fatigue: instant recognition

Danielle struggles with focus late in the test. To fight that, we lower how hard her brain has to work on each question. The easiest way is to train her to instantly recognize the question type and how to approach it. For example, she looks at a Reading question and immediately knows whether it is a main idea question or a comparison question. In Math, she looks and immediately knows whether it is a nonlinear equation, and whether the calculator will help, yes or no. When that recognition is automatic, she spends her energy solving the problem instead of figuring out what she is looking at, and she holds up far better late in the test, especially in the back half of Math where she is losing the most time right now.

Practice, a little every day

She practices about 20 questions a day, five days a week. Each day mixes a few questions from the new skill with questions from everything she has already covered. Practicing daily, and mixing old skills with new ones, is what makes a skill stick instead of fading before test day.

What she needs to hit on each skill:

100%Easy
90%Medium
80%Hard

On her three most common skills (nonlinear equations and functions, command of evidence, words in context) we push the hard target to 85 percent, because they show up so often. Hitting these targets adds about 9 to 11 more questions in Reading and Writing and 12 to 14 in Math, which is the path from about 1125 to about 1400.
