Step 1

How to spot a quadratic

The biggest power is a square, an x² term.

It will ask for the solutions, how many solutions there are, the sum or product of the two answers, or a solution written with a square root in it.

Step 2 · A menu, not a formula to memorize

Pick your tool

  • ✶Does it factor nicely? Factor it, then use the zero-product rule: if two things multiply to make zero, one of them has to be zero. Example: x² + x − 90 = 0.
  • ✶Does it only ask for the sum or the product of the two answers? You do not have to solve. The two answers always add up to −b/a and multiply to c/a.
  • ✶Does it ask how many answers there are, not what they are? Use the discriminant, b² − 4ac. The next slide explains it.
  • ✶Will the answer be messy, like a whole number plus a square root (5 + 3√2)? Use the quadratic formula. The next slide explains it.
  • ✶Is there a repeated piece you can pull out? A shared factor (her Q11) or a perfect square. Pull it out instead of multiplying everything open. That is all "hidden structure" means.
The main by-hand tool

The quadratic formula

When a quadratic does not factor nicely, this always finds the answers. For any equation shaped like ax² + bx + c = 0:

x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a
  • ✶a, b, c are just the numbers in the equation: a sits with the x², b sits with the x, c is the number by itself.
  • ✶The ± means you do it twice, once with a plus and once with a minus. That is what gives the two answers.
  • ✶The part under the square root, b² − 4ac, has a name: the discriminant.
What the discriminant tells youWork it out first and it tells you how many answers before you finish. Positive: two answers. Zero: one answer. Negative: no real answers, because you cannot take the square root of a negative.
The habit · Two examples

When can she use the calculator?

✶ Calculator can do this one
x² − 5x + 6 = 0. What is the larger solution?
All numbers, and it wants a plain number back. Graph it: the curve crosses zero at x = 2 and x = 3. Read off the larger one, 3. The graph hands her the answer.
✶ Calculator cannot finish this one
x² − 10x + 7 = 0 has a solution of the form 5 + 3√n. What is n?
Graphing gives a decimal (about 8.24). That decimal does not tell her n. She needs the algebra to reach the exact answer 5 + 3√2, and only then does she see n = 2. This was her Q4.
The differenceThe first wants a plain number she can read off the graph. The second wants an exact form (a square root kept in) or has a letter in it, and a decimal cannot give her that.

Worked example · Her Q11

y − 42 = (y − c)(y − 42)

  • ✶Step 1. Move everything to one side so it equals zero: (y − 42) − (y − c)(y − 42) = 0
  • ✶Step 2. Rewrite by factoring out the shared piece: (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)] = 0
  • ✶Step 3. Set each factor to zero: y − 42 = 0 and 1 − (y − c) = 0
  • ✶Step 4. Solve each one: y = 42 and y = c + 1 (that is I and III, choice D).
Her trapShe chose 42 and c, treating the right side as already solved and ignoring the (y − 42) on the left.
Her Q11 · Recognizing a shared factor

Two chunks, one shared piece

Move everything to one side: (y − 42) − (y − c)(y − 42) = 0

  • ✶It is two chunks being subtracted: (y − 42) and (y − c)(y − 42).
  • ✶Make the hidden multiplication visible. (y − 42) is the same as 1·(y − 42).
  • ✶Now it reads 1(y − 42) − (y − c)(y − 42), and both chunks contain (y − 42).
  • ✶Pull the shared piece out front: (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)]
Her Q11 · Recognizing a shared factor

Why the inside is [1 − (y − c)]

  • ✶The first chunk 1(y − 42) leaves behind 1.
  • ✶The second chunk (y − c)(y − 42) leaves behind (y − c).
  • ✶The minus sign stays between them: (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)]
Same idea, simpler3x − 5x = x(3 − 5). Both terms share x, so you pull it out and the rest stays inside.
Her Q11 · Recognizing a shared factor

The trick: rename the repeated part

She is not meant to see this instantly. Replace the repeated piece with a single letter.

  • ✶Let A = y − 42.
  • ✶The expression becomes A − (y − c)A.
  • ✶Rewrite the first A as 1A: 1A − (y − c)A.
  • ✶Factor out A: A[1 − (y − c)].
  • ✶Put A back: (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)]
Worked example · Her Q4 · Part 1

Find n, given a solution is 5 + 3√n

The equation: x² − 10x + 7 = 0

  • ✶It does not factor nicely and the answer keeps a square root, so use the quadratic formula.
  • ✶The numbers: a = 1, b = −10, c = 7.
  • ✶Work out the part under the root first: b² − 4ac = (−10)² − 4(1)(7) = 100 − 28 = 72. That is where the √72 comes from.
  • ✶And −b = 10, 2a = 2. So the formula gives:
x = 10 ± √722
Worked example · Her Q4 · Part 2

Simplify the root, divide, then match

x = 10 ± √722

✶Simplify √72. Find a perfect square that divides 72: 72 = 36 × 2. Since √36 = 6, that makes √72 = 6√2.

x = 10 ± 6√22

✶Divide each part on top by 2: 10 ÷ 2 = 5 and 6√2 ÷ 2 = 3√2. So x = 5 ± 3√2.

✶Match it to 5 + 3√n: the 3√2 shows n = 2 (choice A).

Her trapShe set 3√n = √72 and got n = 8, skipping both the simplify (√72 = 6√2) and the divide by 2.
