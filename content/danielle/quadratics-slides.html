Step 1

How to spot a quadratic

A squared variable is the highest power.

It will ask for the solutions, the number of solutions, the sum or product of the roots, or a solution written in a special form.

Step 2 · The decision tree

Pick your tool

  • ✶Factors nicely? Factor and use the zero-product property. x² + x − 90
  • ✶Asks for the sum or product of roots? Sum = −b/a, product = c/a. Do not solve.
  • ✶Asks how many solutions? Use the discriminant b² − 4ac.
  • ✶Ugly numbers, or "in the form a + b√n"? Quadratic formula, then simplify and match.
  • ✶Hidden structure? A common factor or a perfect square. Factor it out, do not expand.
The habit

Calculator or by hand?

Use the calculator only when you can put in real numbers and read a clean root.

Worked example · Her Q11

y − 42 = (y − c)(y − 42)

  • ✶Move everything to one side: (y − 42) − (y − c)(y − 42) = 0
  • ✶Factor out the common (y − 42): (y − 42)[1 − (y − c)] = 0
  • ✶Set each factor to zero: y = 42 and y = c + 1
  • ✶That is I and III, choice D.
Her trapShe chose 42 and c, treating the right side as already solved and ignoring the (y − 42) on the left.
Worked example · Her Q4

x² − 10x + 7 = 0, solution 5 + 3√n

  • ✶Quadratic formula: x = (10 ± √(100 − 28)) / 2 = (10 ± √72) / 2
  • ✶Simplify the radical: √72 = 6√2
  • ✶Divide every term by 2: x = 5 ± 3√2
  • ✶Match: 3√n = 3√2, so n = 2 (choice A).
Her trapShe set 3√n = √72 and got n = 8, skipping both the simplify and the divide by 2.
