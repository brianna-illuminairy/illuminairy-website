Aha Moments

Session 1 · Monday, June 23

  • Find k, not x. When a problem gives a factor like 3x + k instead of asking for x, she factors the expression and reads off the constant — not sets everything to zero.
  • AC method for quadratics. Label a, b, and c; multiply a × c; find two numbers that multiply to that product and add to b — then split the middle term and finish factoring.
  • Break into components. Split a long expression by operator (+/−), simplify each piece, then recombine — keeps sign errors and stalled-out answers from piling up.
  • Distribute the negative first. On equivalent-expression problems with parentheses, she distributes the minus sign before combining like terms.

Session 2 · Wednesday, June 25

  • Reverse FOIL. On a trinomial ax² + bx + c, the squared term comes from F, the constant from L, and the middle term from O + I — use what you know to solve for unknown coefficients.
  • Perfect-square shortcuts. (a ± b)² formulas let her expand or factor squared binomials in one step instead of full FOIL — worth memorizing for timed work.
  • Go the last step. She often gets most of an equivalent-expression problem right but stops before the final combine — finishing the last simplification is now an explicit habit.
