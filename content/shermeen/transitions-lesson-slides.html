The core idea
Concept

A transition names the relationship between two ideas.

Every transitions question gives you two sentences with a word missing between them. Your job is to figure out how the two sentences relate, then pick the word that names that relationship.

Sentence A says

People expected the poem to be boring.

Sentence B says

A critic found it fascinating.

Sentence B contradicts what you'd expect from Sentence A. That relationship is contrast. You need a contrast word.

The most important rule

All four answer choices are grammatically correct.

You cannot pick by what "sounds right." Every option will sound fine in the sentence. You pick by which word names the actual logical relationship between the ideas.

✦Illuminairy
The method
Three steps, every time

Cover the blank. Name the relationship. Match.

1

Cover the answer choices.

Do not look at A, B, C, D yet. If you do, the words bias your reading. You will start trying to make one of them fit instead of figuring out what actually belongs.

2

Read sentence before, read sentence after. Say the relationship in plain words.

Out loud or in your head: "The second sentence contrasts the first." Or: "The second sentence gives an example of the first." Or: "The second sentence is what happens because of the first."

3

Now uncover the choices and find the word that matches.

One choice will be in your category. The other three will be in different categories. If two are in the same category, both are wrong (more on that later).

✦Illuminairy
The four relationships
Memorize these four

Four categories cover almost every question.

A fifth, sequence, shows up less often. We'll get to it.

Contrast

01

The second sentence pushes back on the first. It contradicts it, surprises it, or shows an exception.

however nevertheless nonetheless though yet still on the other hand in contrast instead conversely

Cause & Effect

02

The second sentence is the result of the first. Or the first causes the second.

therefore thus hence consequently accordingly as a result so

Addition / Continuation

03

The second sentence adds to the first. Same direction, more of the same idea, supporting evidence.

moreover furthermore in addition additionally likewise similarly also

Example / Restatement

04

The second sentence shows a specific case of the first, or restates the first in different words.

for example for instance specifically in particular in fact that is in other words
✦Illuminairy
Category 01 of 04
Contrast

When the second idea fights the first.

Signal phrases that tell you the relationship is contrast: "but," "even though," "despite," "while," "you might think X, actually Y."

Example

The forecast called for rain all weekend. However, the sky stayed clear.

Sentence one sets an expectation (rain). Sentence two breaks it (clear sky). Contrast.

Example

Most reviewers panned the film. Nevertheless, it became a cult favorite.

Sentence one suggests failure. Sentence two delivers the opposite. Contrast.

Watch for this setup

"May seem X" or "appears to be X."

When sentence one uses hedging language like "may seem," "appears to be," "at first glance," "you might expect," the writer is setting up a contrast. Sentence two is almost always going to flip it.

Step 1: Read each sentence in plain words

Sentence A: Early critics didn't like her poems.
Sentence B: Later scholars loved the same things the early critics didn't like.

Step 2: Name the relationship

Sentence B reverses sentence A. That's contrast.

Step 3: Match

B "However" is the only contrast word. A is an example word. C is cause and effect. D is addition.

✦Illuminairy
Category 02 of 04
Cause & effect

When the second idea follows from the first.

Read sentence one. Then ask: did sentence two happen because of sentence one? If yes, the relationship is cause and effect.

Example

The bridge inspectors found severe corrosion in the support cables. As a result, the city closed the bridge for repairs.

The closure happened because of the corrosion. Cause then effect.

Example

The new policy required all submissions to be made electronically. Consequently, the office mailroom saw a sharp drop in volume.

The drop in volume is a direct result of the policy. Cause then effect.

The test

Replace the transition with "so" or "because."

If "so" makes the sentence read correctly, you need a cause-and-effect word (therefore, thus, as a result, consequently). If "so" sounds wrong, it is not this category. This is the fastest single check you have.

Plain words

Sentence A: A volcano blocked the sun for a year.
Sentence B: The next year had no summer and crops failed everywhere.

Relationship

The crop failures happened because the volcano blocked the sun. Cause then effect.

Match

C "Consequently" is cause and effect. The test: "The volcano blocked the sun, so 1816 had no summer." That works. Correct.

✦Illuminairy
Category 03 of 04
Addition / continuation

When the second idea piles onto the first.

Both sentences are going the same direction. Sentence two is more of the same idea, more evidence for the same point, or a second item in the same list.

Example

The new library has nearly doubled its book collection in the past decade. Moreover, it has tripled its digital media holdings.

Two facts about the library's growth, both pointing the same way. Addition.

Example

Studies show that regular exercise improves cardiovascular health. Similarly, a diet high in fiber has been linked to lower rates of heart disease.

Two separate health behaviors, both producing the same kind of benefit. Addition.

Warning

"Addition" feels like the safe default. It is also the most common wrong answer.

If both sentences are about the same general topic, your brain wants to call that addition. But "same topic" is not the same as "same direction." Test for contrast and cause first. Only call it addition if sentence two genuinely just adds another piece to sentence one's point.

Quick check · addition
Test the concept

Try it.

Marine biologist Sylvia Earle has spent thousands of hours documenting deep-sea ecosystems that were previously unknown to science. she has founded an organization that has established over 150 marine protected areas worldwide.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A
However,
B
In addition,
C
Therefore,
D
For example,
Plain words

Sentence A: Earle has done a lot of deep-sea research.
Sentence B: Earle has also founded a conservation group.

Relationship

Two separate accomplishments by the same person. Both make her look impressive. Same direction. Addition.

Quick eliminations

Not contrast (nothing fights sentence A). Not cause and effect (the conservation group isn't a result of the research, it's a separate accomplishment). Not example (the conservation work isn't a specific instance of the research).

✦Illuminairy
Category 04 of 04
Example / restatement

When the second idea narrows or restates the first.

Sentence one is general. Sentence two is specific. Or sentence one is one phrasing of an idea, and sentence two is the same idea in clearer words.

Example (narrowing)

Several composers helped define the Romantic era. For instance, Chopin transformed the piano repertoire with his nocturnes and ballades.

General claim, then one specific case. Example.

Example (restatement)

The treaty's terms were nonnegotiable from the outset. That is, neither party was free to alter them after signing.

Same idea, said again in plainer terms. Restatement.

Key distinction

"For example" introduces an example. "That is" and "in other words" restate.

If sentence two is a specific case of sentence one, you want for example, for instance, specifically, in particular. If sentence two repeats sentence one with different words, you want that is, in other words. They're in the same family but they do slightly different jobs.

Bonus category
Sequence / time

When the relationship is "first this, then that."

Less common than the main four, but it shows up. The two sentences describe events in order.

Sequence

Bonus
first then next later subsequently eventually meanwhile previously ultimately finally
The word that catches people

"Ultimately" means "in the end" or "the final outcome."

Not "also" and not "as a result." Use it when sentence two is the eventual outcome of a process, the final stage of what sentence one set up. We're going to see this exact word on one of your missed questions in a minute.

Elimination tricks
Trick 01

If two answer choices mean the same thing, both are wrong.

There can only be one correct answer. If A and B both mean "however," they can't both be right. Since they can't both be right and they mean the same thing, they must both be wrong. Cross them both out.

Example. Look at these four choices without even reading the question.

A
However,
B
Nevertheless,
C
For example,
D
Therefore,
What just happened

A and B are both contrast words. They mean roughly the same thing. So both must be wrong. That leaves C (example) and D (cause and effect). You went from four choices to two without reading a single word of the passage.

✦Illuminairy
Elimination tricks
Trick 02 & 03

Two more shortcuts you can lean on.

Trick 02

"And" and "also" are weak. They rarely win.

SAT transitions test specific relationships. "And" or "also" only signals that another thing exists. If the question is testing a real relationship (contrast, cause, example), one of those specific words will be the answer, not a vague one. Save "and"/"also" as a last resort.

Trick 03

Don't pick by what "sounds right" in your ear.

All four choices will sound grammatical. They are designed to. If you find yourself reading each one out loud trying to "hear" the answer, stop. Go back and name the relationship in plain words first. Then match. Vibe-checking is the single biggest source of wrong answers on this question type.

A note on "ignore the rest of the passage"

You don't need to read the whole long passage. Just read the sentence before the blank and the sentence after. The rest is usually background. If you're getting stuck on whether something is contrast or addition, that usually means you're not actually understanding one of the two sentences. Reread the simpler one.

✦Illuminairy
Secondary strategies
Use when the method needs a shortcut

Three fast moves on transitions questions.

A

Be flexible.

If you need a disagreement word but "however" is not an option, look for another contrast word like "on the other hand."

B

Eliminate copycats.

If two choices do the same job (like "also" and "in addition"), they cannot both be right, so cross both out.

C

Plug and play.

When choices are short, plug each into the blank and read the sentence. Often the winner becomes obvious.

Skip the repeated question stem

On the SAT, transitions questions always ask: "Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?" Once you know that, read the passage and skip re-reading the question every time.

Activity 04 · copycats
Eliminate before you read

Which two choices are the same type?

On the SAT, only one answer can be correct. If two choices do the same job, they cannot both be right, so they must both be wrong. Tap the two words that match, then read why the other two stay in play.

What you are looking for

Two words from the same category.

Not synonyms word-for-word. Same function: both contrast, both cause-and-effect, both example, etc. Cross that pair out before you read the passage. The real answer will be one of the other two.

Step 1: Cover the choices. Read the two sentences in plain words.

Sentence A: The poem looks like there's nothing to say about it.
Sentence B: A critic wrote a whole essay about just its first stanza.

Step 2: Name the relationship

"May seem barren terrain" is the giveaway. The writer is setting up an expectation (this poem is boring) so they can knock it down (a critic found a lot in it). The relationship is contrast.

Step 3: Match

Now uncover the choices.

B "Nonetheless" = contrast. Match.

You picked A "Likewise" on the diagnostic. Likewise is an addition word. It would only work if the second sentence agreed with the first ("the poem seems boring, and similarly, the critics ignored it"). The actual second sentence flips the first. That's contrast, every time.

Medium Expression of Ideas Transitions

When viewing the vibrant, swirling patterns in Maria Lopez's abstract painting series, one's imagination might drift to the colorful nebulae of deep space. it's the series' subtitle, Cosmic Canvas, that grounds the viewer, revealing each piece as a close-up of ordinary paint swatches.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A
Consequently,
B
Alternatively,
C
Ultimately,
D
Additionally,
Step 1: Plain words

Sentence A: When you first look at Lopez's paintings, you imagine deep space.
Sentence B: But the subtitle tells you the paintings are actually close-ups of paint swatches.

Step 2: Name the relationship (this is where it got tricky)

Sentence A is what the viewer thinks first. Sentence B is what the viewer ends up understanding. This is a sequence: first impression → final understanding. That word "ultimately" means "in the end."

Step 3: Match and eliminate

A "Consequently" = cause and effect. The subtitle isn't caused by your imagination. Eliminate.
B "Alternatively" = a different option. The subtitle isn't a different option to imagining nebulae, it's the correction. Eliminate.
C "Ultimately" = the eventual outcome. Sentence B is what the viewer arrives at in the end. Match.
D "Additionally" = more of the same. But sentence B isn't more imagining-nebulae info, it's the reveal that the paintings aren't space at all.

Why you picked D "Additionally"

Sentence B does add new information, so "additionally" feels right. This is the trap from slide 10: same topic ≠ same direction. Sentence B doesn't continue the nebula idea, it corrects it. The relationship is the progression from first impression to final reveal, not the piling-on of more facts.

The rhythmicity study.

Tap your answer when you are ready. Then use Walk through it for the concession breakdown.

Hard Expression of Ideas Transitions

A 2023 study of music learners examined the role of rhythmicity, the predictability of a beat, in music acquisition. The study found that the greater the rhythmicity of a piece, the more likely it was to be mastered quickly. the correlation between acquisition and rhythmicity was weaker than that between acquisition and another factor studied: melody complexity.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A
In fact,
B
In other words,
C
Granted,
D
As a result,
Step 1: Plain words

Sentence A: Higher rhythmicity = faster mastery. (Rhythmicity matters.)
Sentence B: But the link with rhythmicity was weaker than the link with melody complexity. (Rhythmicity matters less than something else.)

Step 2: Name the relationship

Sentence A says rhythmicity matters. Sentence B partially walks that back: yes it matters, but something else matters more. That's a concession, a kind of soft contrast where you admit a limitation to what you just said.

Step 3: Match

A "In fact" = strengthening the previous claim. Sentence B doesn't strengthen sentence A, it weakens it. Eliminate.
B "In other words" = restating in plainer terms. Sentence B is new comparison info, not a restatement. Eliminate.
C "Granted" = admitting a limit or counterpoint. Match.
D "As a result" = cause and effect. The weaker correlation isn't caused by the previous finding. Eliminate.

"Granted" decoded

"Granted" is a concession word. It means "I admit this next thing, which somewhat weakens what I just said." It belongs in the contrast family but it has a softer flavor. Memorize it: when sentence A makes a claim and sentence B says "yeah, but…" the answer is often granted, admittedly, or to be sure.

Why you picked A "In fact"

"In fact" gets misused here because both sentences are about the same study. But "in fact" is for doubling down on what you just said. The second sentence here doesn't double down, it admits a limit. That's the opposite move.

Cheat sheet
Keep this near you while you practice

The whole lesson on one page.

The method
1. Cover the choices. 2. Read both sentences, say the relationship in your own words. 3. Match to one of four categories. Never vibe-check.
Contrast
Sentence B fights sentence A. however, nevertheless, nonetheless, though, yet, still, on the other hand, in contrast, instead, granted, admittedly, that said
Cause & Effect
Sentence B is the result of A. Test: can "so" replace the blank? therefore, thus, hence, consequently, accordingly, as a result
Addition / Continuation
Same direction, more of the same. moreover, furthermore, in addition, additionally, likewise, similarly, also
Example / Restatement
Sentence B is a specific case (example) or the same idea reworded (restatement). for example, for instance, specifically, in particular, in fact, that is, in other words
Sequence / Time
First A, then B. first, then, next, later, subsequently, eventually, meanwhile, previously, ultimately, finally
Copycat elimination
If two choices mean the same thing, both are wrong. Cross them both out before reading the passage.
The trap to remember
Same topic ≠ same direction. Sentence B can be about the same subject as A but still contrast it, conclude it, or correct it. Don't default to "Additionally" just because both sentences are about the same thing.
