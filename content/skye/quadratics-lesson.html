Quadratics: what they are
and how the SAT tests them

A quadratic brings in parabolas, factoring, up to two solutions, and maximum or minimum behavior. We will build the idea step by step, with a short SAT-style question after each piece.

The core idea

The highest power is 2

Standard form
ax2 + bx + c,   where a ≠ 0
The piece that makes it quadratic is the ax2 term. If that term disappears once you simplify, it is not quadratic.

Watch what happens here. It looks quadratic:

x2 + 5x = x2 − 9

Subtract x2 from both sides and the squared term is gone: 5x = −9. That is linear, not quadratic.

What changes once it is quadratic

It can have up to two solutions

A linear equation usually has one solution. A quadratic can have 0, 1, or 2 real solutions.

x2 − 9 = 0  factors as  (x − 3)(x + 3) = 0,  so  x = 3 or x = −3.

Your solving tools: factor, take square roots, complete the square, or use the quadratic formula. The SAT usually rewards spotting when factoring or structure is the fastest route.

The habit that prevents mistakes

If it is an equation, move everything to one side first

Quadratics are easiest to solve when one side is zero. Do not factor before you do this.

1Start with x2 + 5x = 24.
2Move everything left: x2 + 5x − 24 = 0.
3Factor: (x + 8)(x − 3) = 0.
4Solutions: x = −8 or x = 3.

The graph

A quadratic graphs as a parabola

It is not a straight line. It is a U shape or an upside-down U, and the sign of a tells you which.

Example: y = x2 + 3 opens up; y = −x2 + 3 opens down.

On the SAT

Step-by-step solution
1The number in front of x2 is a = −2.

The turning point

The vertex is the maximum or minimum

A line keeps going one direction. A quadratic turns around at one point, the vertex. Vertex form shows it directly:

Vertex form
y = (x − h)2 + k   has vertex   (h, k)
y = (x − 4)2 + 7 has vertex (4, 7). It opens up, so 7 is the minimum value.

SAT questions use this to ask for a maximum value, a minimum value, or when something reaches its highest or lowest point.

On the SAT

Maximum and minimum from vertex form

Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Vertex form gives vertex (4, 7), so x = 4 is where the turn happens.
2(x − 4)2 is never negative, so the smallest f can be is when it equals 0, leaving 7.
3The minimum value is 7. Choice B. The 4 is where it happens, not the value itself.

One quadratic, three outfits

The same quadratic can be written three ways

They describe the same thing, but each form hands you something different for free.

Standard form
x2 − 5x + 6
Shows a, b, c and the direction.
Factored form
(x − 2)(x − 3)
Shows the zeros: x = 2 and x = 3.
Vertex form
(x − 2.5)2 − 0.25
Shows the vertex, and therefore the minimum or maximum: (2.5, −0.25).

A common trap

A visible x2 does not guarantee a quadratic

Simplify first, then decide. Sometimes the squared term divides away or cancels.

Quadratic
x2 + 3x − 4
The squared term stays. This one is quadratic.
Not quadratic
x2x + 3
Simplifies to x + 3. Linear.
Also watch equations: x2 + 4x = x2 + 10 cancels to 4x = 10. Linear, despite the squared terms.

On the SAT

The SAT-level takeaway

When you spot a quadratic, ask

  • Can I rewrite it in standard form, ax2 + bx + c?
  • Can I factor it into (x + m)(x + n)?
  • Is one side equal to zero yet?
  • Are there two possible answers?
  • Is the question about zeros, solutions, maximum, minimum, vertex, or intercepts?

Factoring method 1 · GCF

Pull out the common factor first

Before anything else, check whether every term shares a factor. 3x2 + 12x = 3x(x + 4).

Factoring method 2 · difference of squares

Two squares with a minus between them

a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b).  Example: x2 − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4).

Difference of squares
What is the factored form of 9x2 − 25?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
19x2 = (3x)2, 25 = 52, so a = 3x, b = 5.
2(a − b)(a + b) = (3x − 5)(3x + 5). Choice A.
3B and D are squares, which give a middle term. There is no middle term here.

Factoring method 3 · perfect square, positive middle

First and last are squares, middle is 2ab

a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a + b)2.  Example: x2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)2.

Perfect square trinomial

Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Squares: x2 and 16 = 42. Roots x and 4.
2Middle check: 2 · x · 4 = 8x, positive. Matches.

Factoring method 4 · perfect square, negative middle

Same pattern, with a minus inside

a2 − 2ab + b2 = (a − b)2.  Example: x2 − 6x + 9 = (x − 3)2.

Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Squares: 4x2 = (2x)2, 9 = 32. Roots 2x and 3.
2Middle: 2 · 2x · 3 = 12x, and it is negative.

Factoring method 5 · basic trinomial

Find two numbers for x2 + bx + c

Two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.  Example: x2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

Basic trinomial
What is the factored form of x2 + 7x + 12?

Hint
Step-by-step solution
1Need mn = 12, m + n = 7. Both positive.
23 · 4 = 12 and 3 + 4 = 7. Match. (C adds to 8, D adds to 13.)
3(x + 3)(x + 4). Choice A.

Factoring method 6 · AC method

When the leading coefficient is not 1

For ax2 + bx + c: multiply a · c, find two numbers that multiply to that and add to b, split the middle, then group.

Leading coefficient not 1
What is the factored form of 3x2 + 10x + 8?
Select an answer, then check it or open the steps.
Hint
Step-by-step solution
1ac = 3 · 8 = 24. Two numbers multiply to 24, add to 10: 4 and 6.
2Split: 3x2 + 4x + 6x + 8. Group: x(3x + 4) + 2(3x + 4).
3(3x + 4)(x + 2). Choice A. Check by expanding: 3x2 + 10x + 8.

Run this every time

The quadratic checklist

  • Simplify first. If the x2 survives, it is quadratic.
  • If it is an equation, set one side to zero before factoring.
  • Factor by pattern: GCF, difference of squares, perfect square, basic trinomial, or AC method.
  • Expect up to two solutions, and read the form for what the question wants.
  • Vertex form for maximum or minimum, factored form for zeros, standard form for direction.
