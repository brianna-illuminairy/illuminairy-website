Right triangles

What makes a triangle a right triangle

A right triangle has exactly one 90° angle, shown by the small square in the corner (a triangle can never have two). The two sides that form that corner are the legs; the side across from it, always the longest, is the hypotenuse. Since the three angles total 180°, the two acute angles a and b always add to 90°. Two angles that add to 90° are called complementary.

Triangle basics

Isosceles triangle: equal sides give equal base angles

Isosceles means at least two sides are equal. By the angle-side rule, the two angles across from those equal sides are equal too; these are the base angles. The angle between the two equal sides, up at the top vertex, is the vertex angle. So once you know the vertex angle, the two base angles split whatever is left of 180° evenly.

Lines and angles

A straight line = 180°

A full circle measures 360° all the way around. A straight line is exactly half of that turn, a semicircle, so a straight line measures 180°.

Lines and angles

Cut a straight line and the two angles add to 180°

Draw any line through a point on a straight line. It splits the 180° into two pieces. No matter where you cut, the two pieces always add back to 180°.

Lines and angles

When two lines cross, one angle gives you all four

Now let that cut go all the way through. The two lines make four angles. Angles straight across from each other are vertical angles and are always equal, which is why the two 130° angles match and the two 50° angles match. Any two angles side by side sit on a straight line, so they add to 180°. Put those together and one angle is all you need: know the 130°, and the neighbors are 180 − 130 = 50° while the angle straight across is 130°.

Triangle basics

Interior angles inside; extend a side for an exterior angle

The three angles inside a triangle are its interior angles, and they add to 180°. Extend one side past a corner to make an exterior angle (here ∠ACD). The interior angle and the exterior angle at that corner sit on a straight line, so they add to 180°. Combine the two facts and the exterior angle equals the other two interior angles added: ∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B.

Complementary angles

Complementary means the two angles add to 90°

Two angles are complementary when they add to 90°. In a right triangle this is guaranteed: the right angle already uses 90°, and all three angles must total 180°, so the two acute angles are left to split the remaining 90°. Call them x and y: x + y = 90°.

Complementary angles

Complementary angles

sin x = cos(90° − x)

The sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement, and the cosine equals the sine of its complement. It lets you switch between sine and cosine with no triangle at all.

sin x = cos(90° − x)

cos x = sin(90° − x)

Isosceles right triangle

Isosceles right triangle = 45°, 45°, 90°

Isosceles means two sides are equal. If the two legs of a right triangle are equal, the two angles across from them are equal too (biggest angle faces biggest side, so equal sides give equal angles). Those two angles share the leftover 90°, so each is 45°. Every isosceles right triangle is a 45°-45°-90° triangle. Careful with the wording though: an isosceles triangle does not have to be right. A 70°-70°-40° triangle is isosceles too. Only when an isosceles triangle also has a right angle do you get 45-45-90.
