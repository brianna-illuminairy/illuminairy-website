## The moment I saw it twice.

The first time was my own daughter. She was studying for AP PreCalc — a brand-new exam, with FRQs at the center of it. Varsity Tutors matched her to a man in his seventies who knew math cold but didn't know what an FRQ was, couldn't rotate the PDF she uploaded, and was operating in a universe so far from hers that his expertise was unapplicable to her actual need. She got rematched — this time to a UK college student. Credentialed, but ten thousand miles away from the test her teacher would grade tomorrow. What she actually needed was a college freshman who had scored a 5 on this exam in this country a year ago and could still feel what it took. *Educationally qualified is not the same as one rung ahead and reachable.* I watched that pattern play out, session after session, on a platform I helped run.

The second time was a dentist. Toward the end of my time at Nerdy, more and more of the people calling in were not students. They were adults — professionals and owners — with very discrete outcomes. A dentist who wanted to use AI for lead generation didn't need an academic tutor. He didn't need a random AI generalist either. He needed a plan to implement AI lead-gen for *his* practice, by someone who already understood dental operations and AI implementation, and who would walk it in alongside him. There was no path on our platform to give him that. I pitched the closest version of it — a mentorship program that paired tutors' applied skills with adults' real outcomes. The answer was no. So I left to build it myself.

## Why me.

I'm an engineer. I just left Nerdy/Varsity Tutors — the largest paid 1:1 learning marketplace in the U.S. — where I served as Senior Director of Revenue, running the demand side of one-to-one learning at scale. Before that, I spent six years at Amazon in AI, machine learning, and robotics. In my last performance review at Nerdy, I'd written roughly fifty thousand lines of production code per day — 12.8× the average staff engineer — because I'd rebuilt my workflow around AI. I am the proof point for what an AI-amplified ambitious professional looks like, and I know how rare it is. Most of my senior peers, including excellent engineers, were not using AI this way. Not because they didn't want to. Because no one had walked them through it in their job, on their problems, with someone one rung ahead in their lane. They didn't need a course. They needed what I had: a working pattern, dropped into their context, by someone who had already crossed the same gap.

## Why now.

My earned insight is this: **knowledge is everywhere now, but the plan for applying it is missing.** The person most able to give you that plan, hold you accountable, and get you to a result is not the world-class expert who has never lived your context. It is the person one step ahead of you who has already applied the skill to your exact use case.

> **Applied outcomes > Knowledge.**
> **Near-peer > World-class.**
> **Live one-on-one > Recorded or group.**
> **Interactive joint application > Floundering alone.**

Until now, doing this at scale was impossible — 1:1 was too expensive, near-peer mentors too hard to find, and orchestrating a personalized path live was a job no human marketplace could do. AI changes the economics of every one of those lines. It can find the near-peer match, orchestrate the path live, track progress between sessions, and free the human mentor to do the only thing humans uniquely do: bond, encourage, hold accountable. There is a 12–18 month window where the team that builds this becomes the platform of the next decade. I just left to build it.
