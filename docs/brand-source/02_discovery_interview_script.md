# Customer Discovery Interview Script

> Mom Test format — past behavior only, no pitching
> Status: **v1 — locked 2026-05-19**
> Target: **15 calls in 14 days**

---

## Pre-call hygiene (the Mom Test rules)

1. **Do not pitch IlluminAIry.** They don't need to know you have a startup unless they ask. If they ask, give 1 sentence and pivot back to their experience.
2. **Open script:** *"I'm doing research on how ambitious people are actually trying to apply AI to their work. I'd love to learn from your real experience — 20 minutes, off the record."*
3. **Listen 80%, talk 20%.** Short follow-up prompts only ("Walk me through that.", "What did that look like specifically?", "Then what happened?").
4. **Never lead.** Don't validate. Don't suggest. Don't agree. Just dig.

---

## The 6 questions (all past-behavior, no hypotheticals)

### 1. What you tried
> *"Walk me through the last time you tried to learn or apply AI for something specific in your job or business. What did you actually do, step by step?"*

What I'm listening for: a specific event, a date, a concrete artifact (a course title, a tool, a person they hired, a prompt they wrote). Not "I've been thinking about it."

### 2. What it cost
> *"What did that cost you in money, time, and mental energy? What else did you give up to do it?"*

Real numbers. "$2,400 on a Maven cohort, 8 weekends, time away from my family." Not "a lot."

### 3. Where it broke
> *"Where did it stall, fall short, or fail to give you what you needed?"*

Specific failure point. "I finished the modules but never shipped anything." "The freelancer I hired didn't understand my industry." "I got a certificate but nothing changed in my business."

### 4. What you've paid for in the last 12 months
> *"What have you spent money on in the last year trying to get better at this — courses, coaches, tools, hires, anything? Which ones did you actually finish or use, and which ones rotted on a hard drive somewhere?"*

This is the budget signal and the abandoned-purchase signal. **People who've already paid for failed solutions are gold ICPs.**

### 5. Who helped you (and didn't)
> *"Who, if anyone, actually helped you? What did they do? And what did they not do that you wished they had?"*

This surfaces the human element — the mentor analog. If the answer is "no one" or "ChatGPT", that's a strong signal.

### 6. Snowball + soft commitment
> *"Who else do you know who's stuck on this same kind of problem right now? Could I introduce myself? And — when I have something concrete to show you, can I come back and ask if it would have helped?"*

The number of intros they offer is the closest-to-truth measure of how hair-on-fire this is. Snowball density matters.

---

## Mom Test red flags (compliance check)

If you catch yourself doing any of these mid-call, STOP and reset:

- ❌ Describing IlluminAIry → ✅ Ask another past-behavior question
- ❌ "Would you use a product that…" → ✅ "When have you used something like…"
- ❌ "Do you think this is a problem?" → ✅ "Walk me through the last time it happened"
- ❌ "What would your ideal solution look like?" → ✅ "What did you do when you tried to solve it?"

### Real signals vs. fake signals

**Real (act on):** emotion (frustration, embarrassment, urgency), specific numbers, specific names, unsolicited stories, snowball intros, "can I see it when you have something."

**Fake (discard):** "That's a great idea." "Love it." "You should also add…" "I would totally use that." "Sounds useful." Compliments are noise.
