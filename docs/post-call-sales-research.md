# Post-Call Sales Page Research Dossier (Phase 1: External Only)

- **Date:** 2026-06-13
- **Scope:** External industry research only
- **Question:** What do high-performing teams send prospects to after a sales call when the goal is immediate conversion?
- **Explicit exclusion:** No internal transcripts, CRM tags, or in-repo audience assumptions in this document.

## Method

Reviewed external published research and guidance on:

1. Post-call conversion behavior and timing.
2. Destination page structure for proposal/close pages.
3. Checkout UX and payment completion mechanics.
4. Trust and risk-reduction elements near payment.

## External Findings: What Industry Destination Pages Contain

## 1) Continuity + one concrete next step at top

- The destination page is framed as a continuation of the call, not a fresh browse session.
- The page leads with a single, specific next action, not exploratory navigation.
- Sales-performance research consistently ties explicit next-step clarity to better progression.

**Implication:** opening module should confirm call context and immediate action in one screen.

## 2) Offer clarity before persuasion depth

- Proposal standards from HubSpot/PandaDoc emphasize explicit scope, deliverables, timeline, and pricing.
- High-performing post-call pages are specific before they are persuasive.

**Implication:** page must state exactly what is bought, when it starts, and what happens next before long-form proof.

## 3) Payment-first conversion architecture

- Checkout UX research repeatedly shows abandonment is often caused by friction in the payment flow itself.
- Better performing flows reduce complexity, reduce choices, and foreground the payment action.

**Implication:** payment block should be above the fold and visually dominant on mobile and desktop.

## 4) Transparent pricing and total-cost visibility

- Research repeatedly shows surprise costs and hidden terms increase abandonment.
- Strong destination pages keep pricing and billing expectations explicit near payment.

**Implication:** "pay now" amount and future billing terms should be plain-language and adjacent to checkout action.

## 5) Trust markers at payment moment

- Buyers judge perceived safety visually at point-of-payment.
- Known payment brand cues, "secure checkout" language, and a clean payment container improve confidence.

**Implication:** trust cues belong in/near payment module, not buried in footer.

## 6) Short "what's included" and implementation sequence

- Proposal frameworks converge on explicit deliverables and timeline as core decision drivers.
- Buyers convert faster when they can picture "what happens after I pay."

**Implication:** include a concise "what is included" section and a short post-purchase step sequence.

## 7) Evidence block tied to decision risk

- Industry frameworks include social proof/case evidence as a close aid, not the lead action block.
- Generic claims underperform versus specific outcomes.

**Implication:** include a compact proof section after payment block and included/timeline clarity.

## 8) Minimal objection FAQ at end

- A short objection compression block is common near close pages.
- Effective versions keep 3-5 high-friction questions only.

**Implication:** include concise FAQ for friction topics (billing timing, fit, next steps, support).

## Canonical Destination-Page Blueprint (External Baseline)

1. Continuity header (call recap line + next step)
2. Offer + pricing clarity block
3. Primary action block (embedded payment or sign/accept)
4. What's included
5. Timeline / what happens next
6. Proof (specific outcomes/testimonials)
7. Trust/reassurance cues near payment
8. Short FAQ

## External Confidence Ratings

| Finding | Confidence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Same-day next-step rigor matters | High | Gong large-sample call and deal analyses |
| Payment friction drives abandonment | High | Baymard multi-year checkout research |

## Phase 1 Challenge (Adversarial)

### Challenge A: B2B sales research may not map to parent DTC services

- **Risk:** Gong findings are primarily sales-team/B2B contexts.
- **Decision:** Keep only process-level principles (speed, specificity, next-step clarity), not B2B role assumptions.

### Challenge B: Ecommerce checkout research may overfit product carts

- **Risk:** Baymard findings originate in ecommerce carts.
- **Decision:** Keep friction and trust principles that are channel-agnostic; reject retail-specific patterns (cross-sell cart behaviors).

### Challenge C: Vendor content may have product bias

- **Risk:** Stripe/HubSpot/PandaDoc resources can overstate house solutions.
- **Decision:** Use only claims corroborated across multiple sources or aligned with independent UX research.

### Challenge D: Outdated findings risk

- **Risk:** Some older published figures may drift over time.
- **Decision:** rely on directional pattern consistency over any single absolute stat; focus spec on structural principles that remain stable.

## High-Confidence Rules That Survive Challenge

1. One clear action per destination page.
2. Payment-first layout with explicit current and future billing terms.
3. Visible trust cues at payment block.
4. Concrete included items and next-step timeline.
5. Short proof and FAQ sections after conversion module.
6. Minimal navigation/distraction on close page.
